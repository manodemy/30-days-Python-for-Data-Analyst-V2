Why Option A returns 0 rows:
`NOT IN` expands into multiple `!=` with `AND`. 
If even ONE value in the subquery is `NULL`, `val != NULL` evaluates to UNKNOWN — which silently fails the entire WHERE clause for every single row!

Option B safely filters out NULLs first with `WHERE dept_id IS NOT NULL`.

💡 Pro-tip: In production, avoid `NOT IN` on subqueries and just use `NOT EXISTS`.
